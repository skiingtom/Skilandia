# Skilandia

A ski-resort builder. Fly over Little Hawk Hill, sell tickets, cut runs, build lifts and lodges, then grow into Cedar Ridge and Twin Peaks.

## Play on Windows

Windows builds are paused while the game is being polished. To make one: **Actions → Windows build → Run workflow**, then download **Artifacts → Skilandia-windows** from that run.

1. It contains `Skilandia-Setup-<version>.exe` (installer) and `Skilandia-<version>-portable.exe` (no install, just run it).
2. Double-click it. Windows SmartScreen may say "Windows protected your PC" because the game isn't code-signed yet: click **More info → Run anyway**.

F11 toggles fullscreen.

## The goal: conquer the mountain

The whole game is one big real landscape: a 28 × 17 km valley and the peaks around it, built from a topographic map (1 unit = 1 metre, real elevations from about 500 m to 2,780 m). You start with one small T-bar and two green runs at the base village.

Grow the resort until your lifts reach the high peaks. A peak is **conquered** when a lift's top station reaches its summit (within 350 m, and no more than 150 m below the top). There are 8 peaks, from Hollin Knob (1,916 m) to Kingsfang (2,777 m); conquer them all to win. The **Conquer the mountain** panel lists them; click one to fly there.

Conquering peaks grows the resort:

| Tier | Peaks conquered | Holds at once | Lifts allowed | Village radius |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Local hill | 0 | 2,000 skiers | 10 | 500 m |
| Ski area | 1 | 6,000 skiers | 25 | 800 m |
| Regional resort | 3 | 18,000 skiers | 40 | 1,050 m |
| Destination resort | 5 | 54,000 skiers | 70 | 1,400 m |
| World-class resort | 7 | 162,000 skiers | 110 | 1,800 m |

Becoming a ski area also starts payroll (ski patrol and crew) and unlocks hotels, restaurants, shops, condos and patrol huts.

Skiers only come when the resort looks like it can handle them. The **Skiers** counter shows `on the hill / comfortable load` (hover it for the breakdown). The comfortable load is the smallest of:

- **Lifts**: what your lifts can keep cycling (riders per second × a full lap: ride up, ski down, walk back). Lifts with no run at the top don't count
- **Runs**: room on the snow, about one skier per 7 m of run
- **The tier's maximum** above

How much of that you actually draw depends on how appealing the resort is (variety, food, lodging, price, lift lines). "The hill is saying" tells you whether lifts, runs or the tier is holding you back. Fed-up skiers leave long lines and go home.

Run difficulty uses real-world pitches: green under 25%, blue under 40%, black under 60%, double black above.

## The map

`src/terrain-data.ts` is generated by `python3 tools/terrain/build_heightmap.py` from `tools/terrain/source/overview.webp`, an OpenTopoMap view (map data © OpenStreetMap contributors, SRTM; map style © OpenTopoMap, CC-BY-SA). The script reads elevation from the map's colour tint (hue up to red, then fading saturation up to white), drops contour lines and labels, smooths the result, and writes a 1000 × 594 heightmap (28 m cells). `tools/terrain/preview.png` is a shaded render of it for comparing against the map.

## Controls

- **Open the mountain** on the welcome screen to start
- Right-drag to look, scroll to zoom, WASD to fly (faster the higher you are; Shift to go faster)
- Press **G** (or "Show steepness") to tint the mountain green / blue / black / red by pitch. It turns on automatically while cutting a trail or placing a carpet / T-bar
- Build from the icon bar at the bottom: Look, Trails, Park, Lifts, Base, Food and Resort each open a submenu

### Roads

- A highway runs down the valley, but nothing connects it to your parking lot at the start. **No skiers drive in until a road links the highway to the lot**
- Pick **Roads**, click on the highway (or any road, or the parking lot), click along the way, and end on a road or the lot to finish. Roads cost $6/m, clear the trees in their path, and can't climb steeper than 14%, so zig-zag up steep ground
- Cars drive the route in and out, busier when the hill is busy

### Parking

Every skier arrives by car, so **parking is a fourth limit** on how many skiers come, next to lifts, runs and the resort tier. The lot you start with holds 400 skiers. Build more from the **Roads** menu:

| Parking | Cars | Skiers | Price | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Small lot | 120 | 300 | $4,500 | Gentle ground (≤12%) |
| Large lot | 400 | 1,000 | $13,000 | Gentle ground (≤10%) |
| Parking garage | 2,000 | 5,000 | $90,000 | Unlocks after your first peak; handles slopes up to 20% |

- Parking goes right beside a road (not on it), lines up with the road, and only counts once that road connects to the highway
- Skiers park where there's the most room and walk back to their car to leave
- Lots earn parking fees as they fill up

### Runs

- Five kinds: groomed slope, slalom course (gates), mogul run (one notch harder), terrain park (wide, orange-edged) and glades
- Cutting a run clears the forest in its path. **Glades** are drawn through the trees instead: they're thinned, not cleared, marked with boundary poles, and rate one notch harder
- Skiers only ski on runs. They never cut through the woods
- Park features: rails, boxes and halfpipes go inside a terrain park; jumps go on any run
- Forest covers the mountain up to the treeline (about 1,950 m). Above it, bare rock outcrops take over, more of them the higher you go. Runs can't be cut through rock, so route them down the snow between the outcrops. Lifts can pass over rock

### Edit

- **Clear trees**: click or drag over the forest to fell it ($4 a tree, 60 m brush)
- **Demolish**: click a building, lift, run, park feature or road to take it down. Buildings, lifts and features give 40% back, roads 25%, runs are free to remove (their park features go with them). The highway can't be removed, and you always keep at least one lift. The ground stays cleared where something stood

### Lifts

- Magic carpet, T-bar, double, quad, covered six-pack and 8-person gondola. Click the bottom station, then the top
- Carpets and T-bars ride on the snow, so the ground must climb the whole way (and stay gentle for a carpet). Chairs and the gondola fly over anything uphill
- Select a lift to see its capacity, or take it down for 40% back

### Buildings and staff

- Select a building to move it. Moving costs 25% of its price unless you switch the fee off under ⚙ Settings
- **Base**: lodge, restroom, ski rental, ski school, first-aid clinic, ski patrol hut. Clinics and patrol huts can sit on the mountain
- **Food**: sugar shack, coffee, cafeteria, sandwich shop, après bar, restaurant. Hungry skiers visit the nearest one
- **Resort**: shop, hostel, condo, spa, hotel, heli-ski base. Hostels, spas, bars, hotels, shops, condos and patrol unlock after your first peak
- **Ski school** sells lessons to beginners (some graduate to blues). It needs instructors, who are paid from day one
- **Heli-ski base** ($450,000) flies expert skiers off the map to the backcountry, eight at a time, for $650 a seat. It costs $1,500/min to run plus $600 fuel a flight; a busy one clears about $7,700/min
- Sugar shacks can sit beside trails
- After your first peak, hire ski patrol and a crew

## Develop

```bash
npm install
npm run dev        # game in the browser at http://127.0.0.1:5173/
npm run app        # build, then open it in the desktop window
npm run dist:win   # Windows installer + portable exe in release/ (run on Windows)
npm test           # headless 30-minute playthrough that checks everything works together
```

`npm run dist:win:nowine` builds the Windows app on Linux/macOS without Wine; it skips the icon and the installer, so add `--win zip` to package it as a zip:

```bash
npx electron-builder --win zip --x64 --publish never -c.win.signAndEditExecutable=false
```

The app icon is `build/icon.png`, drawn by `python3 build/make_icon.py`.
